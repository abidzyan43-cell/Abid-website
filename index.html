Lunch

  • Rice or roti
  • Dal

  • Vegetables + salad

Evening

  • Fruit
  • Curd / yogurt
  • Handful of nuts

Dinner

  • Protein source
  • Vegetables
  • Moderate roti/rice according to your goal
Stay hydrated and focus on consistency rather than extreme dieting.

For medical conditions, pregnancy, kidney/liver disease or other special situations, diet should be individualized by a qualified professional.

💪 Fitness Tips

  • Be consistent even when motivation is low.
  • Sleep 7-9 hours whenever possible.
  • Drink enough water.
  • Prioritize protein and whole foods.
  • Do not depend only on supplements.
  • Increase workout intensity gradually.
  • Take rest and recovery seriously.
  • Track your progress.
  • Warm up before intense exercise.
  • Stop exercise if you develop severe pain, chest pain, fainting or breathing difficulty.
Small steps repeated every day create big changes.

🩺 Common Diseases

Select a disease to view basic information.

Hypertension

High blood pressure that can increase cardiovascular risk.

General diet: Reduce excess salt, eat vegetables, fruits, whole grains and appropriate protein. Follow prescribed treatment.

Diabetes

A condition involving elevated blood glucose.

General diet: Prefer high-fiber foods, vegetables, controlled portions and minimize sugary drinks.

Asthma

A chronic condition affecting the airways.

General care: Identify triggers and follow the prescribed inhaler/action plan.

Anemia

A condition in which blood does not carry enough oxygen effectively.

Nutrition: Iron-rich foods such as legumes, leafy vegetables and appropriate animal sources may help depending on the cause.

Malaria

A mosquito-borne infection requiring appropriate diagnosis and treatment.

Nutrition: Maintain fluids and adequate nutritious food during recovery. Treatment must follow medical advice.

Dengue

A viral infection transmitted by mosquitoes.

Care: Fluids and medical monitoring are important. Avoid self-medication with medicines that can increase bleeding risk.

Typhoid

A bacterial infection that requires appropriate medical treatment.

Diet: Fluids and easily tolerated nutritious foods may be useful during recovery.

Tuberculosis

An infectious disease commonly affecting the lungs.

Nutrition: Adequate calories and protein can support recovery alongside the complete prescribed treatment.

Obesity

Excess body fat that can increase health risks.

Diet: Focus on sustainable calorie control, protein, vegetables, fiber and regular activity.

Migraine

A neurological condition that can cause recurrent headaches and other symptoms.

General care: Regular sleep, hydration and identifying individual triggers may help.

Gastritis

Inflammation of the stomach lining.

Diet: Avoid foods that clearly trigger symptoms and follow medical advice.

GERD

Acid reflux from the stomach into the esophagus.

General care: Avoid large late-night meals and individual trigger foods.

UTI

Urinary tract infection requiring appropriate assessment and treatment.

General care: Maintain hydration unless a clinician has advised fluid restriction.

Arthritis

A group of conditions causing joint pain and inflammation.

General care: Appropriate activity, healthy weight and individualized treatment can help.

High Cholesterol

Elevated blood lipids that can contribute to cardiovascular risk.

Diet: Increase fiber-rich foods and reduce excessive saturated and trans fats.

Fatty Liver

Excess fat accumulation in the liver.

General care: Weight management, physical activity and limiting alcohol when relevant are important.

Kidney Stones

Hard deposits that can form in the kidneys.

General care: Adequate fluid intake is commonly important, but the exact diet depends on stone type.

Constipation

Infrequent or difficult bowel movements.

Diet: Gradually increase fiber, fluids and physical activity when appropriate.

Diarrhea

Frequent loose or watery stools.

Care: Replace fluids and electrolytes. Seek medical care for severe dehydration, blood in stool or persistent symptoms.

Common Cold

A common viral respiratory infection.

Care: Rest, fluids and symptomatic care can help. Seek medical attention for severe or unusual symptoms.

Consistency Over Motivation

Motivation can get you started, but consistency keeps you moving. You will not feel motivated every day. The real transformation happens when you continue even on the days when motivation is low.

“Success is not about doing something once. It is about showing up again and again.”

Discipline

Discipline means doing what needs to be done even when you don't feel like doing it. Small daily actions eventually become habits.
